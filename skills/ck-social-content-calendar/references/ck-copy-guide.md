# Comfort Keepers Social Media Copywriting Skill Guide

## Purpose

Use this guide to plan and write clear, engaging, human-sounding social
media copy for Comfort Keepers.

The goal is to create content that: - Speaks to seniors and the family
members who help make care decisions. - Explains services in plain,
practical language. - Shows how in-home care can support everyday life
and independence. - Recognises real family concerns without creating
fear or guilt. - Gives readers a clear reason to learn more or get in
touch.

The writing should sound like a thoughtful person, not a generic
advertisement or an AI-generated caption.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Brand and audience foundation

### Brand idea

Comfort Keepers' brand promise is **Elevating the Human Spirit**.

A key part of its care philosophy is **Interactive Caregiving**: doing
things *with* seniors, rather than simply doing everything *for* them.
The approach should respect each person's abilities, preferences,
routines, and independence.

### Primary audiences

1.  **Adult children and family members**
    -   Looking for care options for an aging parent.
    -   Worried about a parent living alone or managing daily tasks.
    -   Balancing work, family, and caregiving.
    -   Unsure what type of care is appropriate or when to seek help.
2.  **Seniors**
    -   Want to remain comfortable and independent at home.
    -   May need help with certain daily activities.
    -   Want companionship and opportunities to stay involved.
3.  **Family caregivers**
    -   Need practical support and time to recharge.
    -   May be managing ongoing responsibilities alongside other
        commitments.
4.  **Caregivers and local communities**
    -   Interested in caregiving careers, recognition, local events, and
        community involvement.

### Tone of voice

-   Warm, respectful, reassuring, and practical.
-   Clear and conversational.
-   Positive without being overly sentimental.
-   Confident but never pushy.
-   Focused on seniors as people with preferences, interests, and
    abilities.

### Avoid

-   Infantilising seniors or portraying them as helpless.
-   Making family members feel guilty for needing help.
-   Fear-based statements or exaggerated urgency.
-   Generic phrases that could belong to any care brand.
-   Overpromising outcomes or suggesting every service is available in
    every location.
-   Invented statistics, testimonials, client stories, or service
    details.
-   Repeating the same emotional angle in every post.
-   Em dashes.

## 2. Core copywriting principles

### Lead with the reader's situation

Start with something the audience recognises: a concern, a question, a
daily challenge, or a goal.

### Be specific

Instead of saying:

> We're here to make life better.

Say:

> Need help with meals, errands, or everyday activities? Explore in-home
> care options that fit your loved one's needs.

### Explain the service, not just its name

Do not assume readers know what companion care, respite care, or
personal care means. Explain what the service may involve and when a
family might consider it.

### Focus on practical value

Show how support can fit into real life: preparing meals, getting ready
for the day, attending appointments, enjoying conversation, or giving a
family caregiver time to rest.

### Keep the message focused

One creative should communicate one main idea. Avoid trying to explain
every service or benefit in one post.

### Use natural calls to action

Examples: - Learn more about in-home care. - Talk with your local
Comfort Keepers team. - Explore care options for your loved one. - Ask
us how we can help. - Get in touch to discuss your family's needs.

Use the CTA that matches the post. Not every post needs a sales-focused
CTA.

## 3. Content types and copy approaches

These are the main content types to rotate through. They are not
separate rigid templates. A post can combine two approaches when it
makes sense.

### A. Service education and explanation

**Purpose:** Help people understand what a service is, what it may
include, and who may find it useful.

Useful formats: - What is this service? - What's included? - How does it
help? - When might a family consider it? - A service in everyday life. -
Frequently asked questions. - Service comparison or choosing care.

Example:

**Headline:** What Does Companion Care Actually Include?

**Supporting copy:** Companion care can include conversation, shared
activities, help with everyday routines, and support with errands. It is
a way to help seniors stay connected and engaged.

**CTA:** Learn more about companion care.

### B. Pain-point and concern-led copy

**Purpose:** Speak to a concern that a senior or family member may
already be experiencing. This is one content pointer within the wider
content mix, not the entire strategy.

Possible angles: - Concern about a parent living alone. - Difficulty
managing daily activities. - Worry about meals or nutrition. - Balancing
work and caregiving. - Not knowing when additional care may help. -
Finding time for personal responsibilities while caring for a parent.

Examples:

-   Worried About How Mom Is Managing on Her Own?
-   Is Dad Finding Everyday Tasks More Difficult?
-   Who Helps Mom When You're at Work?
-   Not Sure When It's Time to Consider In-Home Care?
-   Feeling Overwhelmed by Your Caregiving Responsibilities?
-   Looking for the Right Care Plan for Your Aging Parent?

Supporting copy should acknowledge the concern, introduce a relevant
service or support option, and provide a clear next step.

Example:

**Headline:** Worried About Mom Living on Her Own?

**Supporting copy:** If everyday tasks are becoming more difficult, a
little extra support at home may help. Explore care options based on her
needs and routines.

**CTA:** Talk with your local Comfort Keepers team.

Important: Never use fear, shame, or guilt to pressure families into
enquiring.

### C. Situation-led copy

**Purpose:** Describe a recognisable moment from everyday family life.

Examples:

**Headline:** You Can't Always Be There. But You Can Explore Support.

**Supporting copy:** Work and family commitments can make it difficult
to be present every day. In-home care may provide additional help with
daily activities and companionship.

**Headline:** Is Getting to Appointments Becoming Difficult for Dad?

**Supporting copy:** Transportation support may help seniors get to
appointments, run errands, and stay connected with their communities.

### D. Benefits-led copy

**Purpose:** Explain the practical value of a service.

Examples: - More Support With Everyday Routines - Help With Meals,
Without Losing the Joy of Mealtime - A Little Company Can Make the Day
More Engaging - Support for Seniors. Time to Recharge for Family
Caregivers.

Do not make unsupported promises. Use language such as "can help", "may
support", and "designed to" where appropriate.

### E. Pointer, listicle, and checklist copy

**Purpose:** Give readers useful, easy-to-scan information.

Examples: - 5 Ways to Make Everyday Life Easier for Seniors - 4 Ways
Families Can Support Aging Parents - A Simple Home Safety Checklist for
Seniors - 5 Activities to Keep Seniors Engaged at Home - Small Ways to
Make Mealtimes More Enjoyable - Signs Your Loved One May Need Additional
Support - 4 Ways to Make Caregiving More Manageable

Pointers should contain genuinely useful information. Do not add a
numbered list just to make a headline look engaging.

### F. Question-led copy

**Purpose:** Start a conversation or invite readers to reflect.

Examples: - What Does a Good Day at Home Look Like for Your Loved One? -
Have You Talked With Your Parents About Their Care Preferences? - What
Activities Does Your Loved One Enjoy Most? - Are You Familiar With the
Different Types of In-Home Care?

Use open questions for engagement and more specific questions for
service education or enquiries.

### G. Reassurance-led copy

**Purpose:** Reduce uncertainty around care decisions without making
guarantees.

Examples: - Finding Care for a Loved One Can Feel Like a Big Decision.
Start With a Conversation. - The Right Support Starts With Understanding
What Matters to Your Family. - Getting Help at Home Does Not Have to
Mean Giving Up Every Part of Your Routine.

### H. Interactive Caregiving and independence

**Purpose:** Communicate the brand's care philosophy: doing things with
seniors, not simply for them.

Examples: - Care Is Better When We Do It Together. - Everyday Activities
Can Still Be Meaningful Moments. - Support That Encourages Seniors to
Stay Involved. - A Shared Meal Can Be About More Than What's on the
Plate. - Helping Seniors Stay Connected to the Activities They Enjoy.

Show seniors participating, making choices, and engaging in activities.
Avoid visuals or copy that suggest seniors have no agency.

### I. Family caregiver support and respite

**Purpose:** Recognise the responsibilities of family caregivers and
explain that support can benefit the whole family.

Examples: - Who Takes Care of the Caregiver? - You Deserve Time to
Recharge, Too. - Caring for Someone You Love Is Important. So Is Taking
a Break. - Respite Care Can Give Family Caregivers Time to Rest and
Attend to Other Responsibilities.

### J. Wishes and occasion-based content

**Purpose:** Mark holidays, awareness days, celebrations, and community
occasions in a way that feels relevant to the brand.

Possible approaches: - Warm and heartfelt. - Short and elegant. -
Community-focused. - Appreciation-led. - Reflective. - Celebratory. -
Informative when the occasion has a specific meaning.

Examples:

**Thanksgiving:** Wishing you a Thanksgiving filled with meaningful
moments, good company, and time with the people who matter most.

**New Year:** Here's to a new year filled with connection, meaningful
moments, and new memories.

**Grandparents Day:** Celebrating the stories, wisdom, and memories that
bring generations together.

**Caregiver appreciation:** Recognising the compassion, patience, and
dedication caregivers bring to the people they support.

For remembrance and awareness occasions, understand the meaning before
writing. Do not treat Memorial Day and Veterans Day as interchangeable.

### K. Community and local content

**Purpose:** Make the content feel connected to the local office and the
people it serves.

Examples: - Local event participation. - Community partnerships. -
Caregiver recognition. - Office announcements. - Local service
reminders. - Relevant seasonal guidance.

Use verified local details, correct phone numbers, and approved office
information.

### L. Caregiver recruitment and appreciation

**Purpose:** Recognise caregivers and communicate what caregiving work
involves.

Examples: - Make a Difference in Someone's Everyday Life. - Bring
Compassion to a Career That Matters. - Celebrating the People Who Make
Care Personal. - A Thank You to Our Dedicated Caregivers.

Do not make unverified claims about pay, benefits, schedules, or job
availability.

### M. Myth versus fact

**Purpose:** Address common misunderstandings about aging, home care,
and caregiving.

Examples: - Myth: Asking for help means giving up independence.\
Fact: The right support may help seniors continue participating in
everyday life.

-   Myth: Home care is only for people who need constant assistance.\
    Fact: Care needs vary, and some people may benefit from support with
    only certain activities.

Check the accuracy of every myth and fact before publishing.

### N. FAQ and educational content

**Purpose:** Answer questions families commonly have when exploring
care.

Examples: - What Is the Difference Between Companion Care and Personal
Care? - Can In-Home Care Be Scheduled Around a Family's Routine? - When
Should a Family Consider Respite Care? - What Should We Discuss Before
Choosing a Care Provider?

Answers must reflect the actual services and policies of the relevant
local office.

### O. Perspective-based and storytelling copy

**Purpose:** Make content relatable through a clearly defined point of
view or a small everyday moment.

Possible perspectives: - Adult child considering care for a parent. -
Senior describing what independence means to them. - Family caregiver
balancing responsibilities. - Caregiver explaining the value of shared
activities.

Use fictional scenarios only when clearly presented as illustrative.
Never imply an invented story is a real Comfort Keepers client
experience.

## 4. Explaining Comfort Keepers services

Use this three-step structure for every service:

1.  **What is it?** Explain the service in plain language.
2.  **What may it involve?** Give examples of practical activities.
3.  **When might it help?** Describe a situation where a family may
    consider it.

The examples below are starting points only. Confirm the exact service
scope and availability with the local office before publishing.

### Companion Care

**What it is:** Support that helps seniors enjoy companionship and stay
engaged in everyday life.

**May involve:** Conversation, shared activities, hobbies, walks where
appropriate, and help with errands or routines depending on the local
service offering.

**Possible headlines:** - More Than Company. Meaningful Moments
Together. - Is Mom Spending Too Much Time Alone? - Companionship That
Helps Make the Day More Engaging.

### Personal Care

**What it is:** Assistance with personal daily activities while
respecting the individual's preferences and dignity.

**May involve:** Support with bathing, dressing, grooming, mobility, or
other personal routines, depending on the care plan and local offering.

**Possible headlines:** - A Helping Hand With Everyday Personal Care. -
Is Getting Ready for the Day Becoming Difficult? - Personal Care That
Respects Individual Needs.

### Respite Care

**What it is:** Temporary support that gives family caregivers time to
rest or attend to other responsibilities.

**May involve:** A caregiver stepping in for an agreed period to provide
support included in the care plan.

**Possible headlines:** - Family Caregivers Need Support, Too. - When
You Need Time to Recharge, Respite Care Can Help. - Who Steps In When
You Need a Break?

### Dementia Care

**What it is:** Support for people living with dementia, shaped around
their needs, preferences, and routines.

**May involve:** Familiar activities, companionship, routine-based
support, and assistance with daily activities, according to the care
plan and caregiver training.

**Possible headlines:** - Looking for Support for a Loved One Living
With Dementia? - Familiar Routines Can Make a Difference. - Care That
Recognises the Person Beyond the Diagnosis.

Avoid implying that dementia care reverses, cures, or stops the
progression of dementia.

### Meal Preparation and Nutrition Support

**What it is:** Help with meal-related tasks and mealtime routines.

**May involve:** Meal planning, preparation, and support during meals,
subject to the individual's needs and the local service scope.

**Possible headlines:** - Are Your Parents Getting the Mealtime Support
They Need? - Making Everyday Mealtimes Easier. - Good Food. Familiar
Routines. A Little Extra Help.

Do not provide medical nutrition claims or promise a particular health
outcome.

### Transportation and Errands

**What it is:** Support that helps seniors get to places and complete
everyday errands.

**May involve:** Transportation to appointments, shopping, or other
activities, where offered by the local office.

**Possible headlines:** - Is Getting to Appointments Becoming
Difficult? - Helping Seniors Stay Connected to Their Communities. -
Support for the Places Life Takes You.

### Light Housekeeping

**What it is:** Help with selected household tasks to support a
comfortable living environment.

**May involve:** Light cleaning, laundry, or tidying, depending on the
care plan and local offering.

**Possible headlines:** - A Little Help Around the House Can Go a Long
Way. - Is Keeping Up With Household Tasks Becoming Difficult? - More
Time for the Things You Enjoy.

### 24-Hour Care

**What it is:** An arrangement for ongoing care coverage across the day
and night, where available and appropriate.

**May involve:** Caregiver support arranged according to assessed needs
and the agreed care plan.

**Possible headlines:** - Does Your Loved One Need Support Throughout
the Day and Night? - Exploring Around-the-Clock Care for Your Family. -
Care Arrangements Built Around Changing Needs.

Confirm whether the local office offers 24-hour, live-in, or other
overnight arrangements. These terms are not automatically
interchangeable.

## 6. Creative copy versus caption

### Creative copy

The creative should communicate the main idea quickly.

Usually include: - One clear headline. - A short supporting line, if
needed. - A small number of useful pointers, if the format calls for
them. - A relevant CTA, where appropriate.

Do not overcrowd the design with the full caption.

### Caption

The caption should add context rather than repeat the creative word for
word.

A useful structure: 1. Open with a related observation or context. 2.
Explain the service, idea, or practical benefit. 3. Connect it to the
reader's situation. 4. Add a natural CTA.

Example:

**Creative headline:** Worried About Mom Living on Her Own?

**Caption:** If everyday tasks are becoming harder to manage, extra
support at home may help. Comfort Keepers offers care options designed
around individual needs and routines. Contact your local team to learn
more.

## 8. Suggested monthly content mix

For a typical month of 12 to 14 social creatives, use a balanced mix
rather than repeating one type of post.

A possible mix:

-   2 service education posts.
-   2 pain-point, family concern, or care decision posts.
-   2 Interactive Caregiving or senior independence posts.
-   1 family caregiver support or respite post.
-   1 practical tips, checklist, or pointer post.
-   1 senior wellness or home safety post.
-   1 caregiver appreciation or recruitment post.
-   1 local or community post.
-   1 seasonal wish, awareness, or occasion post when relevant.

Adjust the mix to local priorities, seasonal dates, approved services,
and the client's feedback. Not every month needs every category.

## 9. Visual and production guidance

Follow the current client brief and approved brand assets. For the usual
social creative format:

-   Use the specified 4:5 canvas, 1080 × 1350 px.
-   Follow the current Comfort Keepers brand guidelines, fonts, and
    colours.
-   Use warm, realistic imagery featuring seniors and caregivers.
-   Show seniors with dignity, personality, and agency.
-   Prefer natural interactions and everyday settings.
-   Avoid visuals that make seniors look helpless, isolated, or
    childlike.
-   Keep headlines readable and avoid overcrowding.
-   Leave clean natural negative space for the approved logo to be added
    manually when required.
-   Do not create a fake logo, placeholder logo, or logo box.
-   Vary layouts and visual treatments. Do not make every creative dark
    or use the same template repeatedly.
-   Use the correct local phone number and website only when the post
    requires them.

## 10. Accuracy and approval checklist

Before publishing, confirm:

### Message

-   Is the main point immediately clear?
-   Does the headline speak to a real need, question, or interest?
-   Is the copy specific rather than generic?
-   Does the supporting copy add something useful?
-   Is the CTA appropriate?

### Brand

-   Is the tone warm, respectful, and practical?
-   Does the content support independence and Interactive Caregiving?
-   Does it avoid guilt, fear, and patronising language?

### Service accuracy

-   Is the service actually offered by the local office?
-   Are the activities and inclusions accurate?
-   Are scheduling, availability, and care arrangements confirmed?
-   Are all phone numbers, locations, and links correct?
-   Are statistics, claims, and testimonials verified?

### Creative quality

-   Is the copy easy to read at social-media size?
-   Is the design uncluttered?
-   Is the image appropriate and realistic?
-   Is the layout varied from recent posts?
-   Does the creative follow the required dimensions and brand
    guidelines?

### Final copy test

Ask:

> Would a real family member understand this immediately, recognise why
> it matters, and know what to do next?

If not, simplify and make the message more specific.

## 11. Reusable copywriting workflow

For each new post:

1.  **Choose the audience:** senior, adult child, family caregiver,
    caregiver applicant, or local community.
2.  **Choose the content type:** service education, pain-point,
    situation-led, pointer, wish, FAQ, community, and so on.
3.  **Choose one message:** do not combine several unrelated ideas.
4.  **Choose the angle:** question, concern, practical benefit,
    explanation, encouragement, or celebration.
5.  **Write the headline:** make the topic clear and natural.
6.  **Add supporting copy:** explain the point in plain language.
7.  **Add a CTA:** only if it helps the reader take a next step.
8.  **Verify details:** confirm service scope, local availability, phone
    number, and claims.
9.  **Review for repetition:** compare with recent posts and change the
    angle or format if needed.
10. **Check the creative:** ensure the words are readable and fit the
    design.

## Guiding principle

**Start with the person, not the service name.**

Understand what the senior or family member may be experiencing. Then
explain the relevant support in a clear, respectful, and practical way.

The strongest Comfort Keepers content should help people understand
care, see how support may fit into everyday life, and feel comfortable
taking the next step.
