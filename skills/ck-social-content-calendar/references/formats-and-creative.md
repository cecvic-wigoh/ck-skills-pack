# Formats, on-creative copy and voice (Comfort Keepers social)

Distilled from a finished, approved calendar (CK Lexington, Dec 2026). Office-specific facts are removed; fill them per office.

## Formats
- **Static**: one picture with a few big words. 1080x1350 (4:5). Use for one clear message.
  On-creative copy: headline (6 words or fewer), subline (one short line), button text (e.g. "Call (XXX) XXX-XXXX").
- **Carousel**: 3 to 7 swipeable slides, 1080x1350. Use for steps, lists, "what a visit looks like".
  One idea per slide; slide 1 is the hook; last slide is the CTA with the phone number. Slide counter top right, "Swipe →" pill bottom right.
- **Reel**: short upright video, 15 to 30 s, 1080x1920 (9:16). Use to show a moment; feelings land best on video.
  Deliver: timed shot list (beat, seconds, shot, on-screen text), voiceover script in plain speech, closing card (office name, phone, franchise line). Keep key text between y=250 and y=1250 to clear the Reels UI. Cover frame has a 4 to 6 word headline.
- Suggested monthly mix: about 40% statics, 30% carousels, 30% reels.

## Creative layout rules
- Text is set over the photo (HTML/CSS), never generated inside the image. Phone number letter-perfect.
- Headline serif bold 88 to 104 px on a 1080 canvas; subline sans semibold 44 to 50 px; button sans extra-bold 52 to 54 px; nothing under 28 px.
- High contrast: warm cream panels, deep navy text and buttons, one small warm accent. Swap for the office's official colors and logo when supplied; until then use a plain text wordmark "Comfort Keepers of <City>".
- Photography: warm, real-looking homes in the office's region; older adults in their 70s to 80s, relaxed or laughing, real skin texture; caregiver in plain clothes; soft natural light; seasonal touches only in season. Avoid hospital settings, wheelchairs as focal point, sad or lonely hero shots, clinical blue, stock thumbs-up.
- AI images are stand-ins only. Replace with real office photos with signed consent, or follow Meta's AI-label rules.

## Voice rules for readers aged 60 to 80
1. Talk to "you". The senior is the reader, not "your loved one".
2. Short sentences, mostly under 12 words. One idea per line.
3. Plain words a neighbor would use. No industry terms or acronyms.
4. Name the hard thing once, calmly, then move to what helps. No fear, no urgency.
5. Say who's in charge: you are. You choose the help, the hours, the person.
6. Be honest about limits: non-medical care; caregivers remind about medicine, they don't give it.
7. One CTA per post, usually a phone call, with the number written out in full.
8. Image text 6 words or fewer, very large, high contrast.
9. Close with the franchise line the office requires (default: "Each office is independently owned and operated.").

## Say this, not this
| Say this | Not this |
|---|---|
| help at home | in-home care solutions |
| a caregiver comes to your house | care professional services |
| you, your home, your way | your loved one (when talking to the senior) |
| older adults, folks, neighbors | elderly, senior citizens, patients |
| help getting dressed, bathing, the bathroom | ADLs, activities of daily living |
| good company, someone to talk to | socialization, engagement programming |
| stay steady, a fall or a close call | fall risk, decline, frail |
| a little help, start small | intervention, placement |
| free, no pressure | limited time, act now, don't wait |
| non-medical; works alongside your doctor | treats, cures, medical care |
| your own home | aging in place |

## Community management (include in strategy)
Reply to every comment within an hour in the same plain voice; move personal or health details to a private message or call; never discuss a client publicly; log calls that mention social.
